Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 September 2026

Walden Hollow is a private record-keeping app for fishing clubs. This policy explains what it collects, why, who can see it, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be read, not to be survived.

The short version. We collect what the app needs to keep your club's fishing record and nothing else. Your data is visible to your own club and to nobody else's. We do not sell it, share it for advertising, or use it to train anything. You can delete your account and your data from inside the app.

Who we are

Walden Hollow is built and operated by Chad Zimmerman, in Colorado, United States. For any privacy question — including a request to see, correct or remove your data — write to chadzimmerman.codes@gmail.com. That address is read by a person, and it is the fastest way to reach one.

Location, specifically

The app asks for location only while it is open, and only to answer one question: which hole are you standing at. We do not track your movements, we do not keep a history of where your phone has been, and we do not run location in the background.

What is stored is the hole you attach to a catch, which is the club's own named location, chosen by you.

This website itself

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and makes no requests to any third party. The fonts and images are served from this site rather than from a font or CDN provider, so visiting this page does not report your visit to anybody.

What we deliberately do not collect

  • No advertising identifiers, and no advertising of any kind
  • No third-party analytics or behavioural tracking
  • No contacts, calendar, microphone, or health data
  • No background location

Who your data goes to

We use a small number of services to run the app. They process data on our behalf; none of them are permitted to use it for their own purposes.

  • Google Firebase for sign-in, the database, and photo storage. This is where your club's record physically lives.
  • Mapbox for map imagery. Your club's hole locations are drawn on your device; they are not uploaded to Mapbox.
  • U.S. Geological Survey and the National Weather Service for public river-gauge and weather readings. These are public feeds; the app reads from them and sends nothing to them.

We do not sell your data, we do not share it for advertising, and we do not use your photographs or records to train machine-learning models belonging to anyone else.

Fish identification

Photographs of fish may be used to build your club's own record of individual fish, and to improve how well identification works for your club. Photographs are not published, not shared with other clubs, and not used for anything outside the product.

If a club chooses to contribute anonymised data to a fisheries research partnership, that is a decision the club makes explicitly. It never happens automatically, and precise hole locations are never included.

How long we keep it

Your club's fishing record is kept for as long as the club uses the app. That is the point of a record. If you delete your account, see below. If a club leaves, we export the club's record, hand it over, and delete our copy.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Delete My Account. The app shows you exactly what follows and asks for your password before anything happens. It cannot be undone.

What is deleted:

  • Your sign-in, so you cannot open the app again.
  • Your phone number and contact email, removed from the club roster.
  • Your profile photograph.

What is not, and this is a genuine trade-off worth stating plainly:

The fish you logged stay in the club's record, with your name on them, and so do the photographs you attached to them.

That is deliberate rather than an oversight. A club's catch log is its account of its own water over years, and a catch with the angler removed is not an anonymous catch, it is a hole in the record. It is the same reason a member whose access an administrator ends keeps their name on their catches. The app says all of this on screen, before it asks for your password, because it is not something we can offer to undo afterwards.

If you would rather your catches and their photographs were removed entirely, ask. Email chadzimmerman.codes@gmail.com, and we will do it and confirm when it is done. That is a request we honour; it is simply not what the in-app button does, and we would rather say so than have you find out later.

One technical detail, because it affects what deletion can mean. Photographs are served through links that carry their own access token. Removing someone's access stops them browsing or fetching new photographs; it does not reach back and invalidate a link somebody already holds. We will not tell you otherwise.

The demo account

The app publishes a demo sign-in — demo@waldenhollow.com, password password — so that anyone can see what the app does without an account. Both app stores require a working sign-in for review, and there is no public registration.

Publishing a password is safe here because the account does not exist. There is no user record and no stored data behind it: the app recognises those credentials before it contacts any server, and then runs on invented sample data built into the app itself. A demo session never signs in to anything, never reads a real club's records, and saves nothing. Whatever a demo visitor does is gone when they close the app.

Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of your data, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. Write to chadzimmerman.codes@gmail.com and we will respond within 30 days. Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights under laws such as the GDPR or the CCPA; we apply the same standard to everyone regardless.

Children

The app is for club members and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. A club member under 18 should have a parent or guardian's agreement before joining.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest by our hosting provider. Access to your club's records is enforced on the server side. The rules that decide who may read what are tested automatically on every change to the app, because a mistake there is the failure that would matter most.

No system is perfect. If we ever discover a breach affecting your data, we will tell you and the relevant authorities as required by law.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what we collect or who can see it, we will say so in the app rather than quietly updating this page.
